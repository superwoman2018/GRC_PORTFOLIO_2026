# 03 — EU AI Act High-Risk Assessment

**Framework:** EU AI Act
**Core skill demonstrated:** Translating regulation to operational reality —
correctly classifying AI systems, including the ones that look high-risk but
aren't, and the ones that look fine but aren't.

## What's in this folder

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `scenario.md` | Why an inventory first, and the reasoning behind each classification |
| `deliverables/CloudNative_AI_System_Inventory.xlsx` | All 4 of CloudNative's AI systems, each classified into a risk tier with reasoning |
| `deliverables/TalentMatch_AI_High_Risk_Assessment.docx` | Full worked classification and obligations breakdown for the one High-Risk system |

## How to read this

Start with the inventory to see the full spread — one High-Risk system, one
Limited Risk, two Minimal Risk — then open the TalentMatch AI assessment to see
a complete classification argument: the exact Annex III clause it falls under,
why the "no auto-reject" design doesn't change that, the full obligation
breakdown, and the specific gaps against those obligations today.

The systems classified as *not* high-risk in the inventory are argued in less
depth there, but the reasoning for each is written out — see `scenario.md`
for why those calls (especially the internal anomaly-detection tool) needed as
much thought as the obvious high-risk case did.

## Disclaimer

This project is a portfolio exercise for a fictional company — it is not legal
advice and was not produced by a lawyer. The EU AI Act's Annex III scope,
obligations, and compliance timelines are active, evolving legislation; treat
the specific article citations and deadlines here as illustrative of the
*type* of reasoning involved, not as current legal fact. Verify anything here
against the official EU AI Act text and current guidance before relying on it
for a real compliance decision.
